 Page 1 — Title page

Illustration: Title page. Sunset over green hills. Aarav sits cross-legged reading; Anya laughs beside the pink piggy bank; the red bicycle leans on a mango tree.

Text: The Little Money Makers — A Journey to a Smarter Future. Small Lessons. Big Dreams. A Smarter Future.

 Page 2 — Meet Aarav

Illustration: Living room. The whole family stands on a teal rug: Dad, Aarav waving, Anya, Mum. Family photo and shelf on the wall.

Speech bubbles: “Hi! I’m Aarav. Want to come on an adventure?” · “Me too!”

This is Aarav. He is eight years old, and he is full of questions. Why is the sky blue? How do aeroplanes fly? And where does money come from?

Aarav lives with Mum, Dad and his little sister, Anya, who is six. Anya loves to laugh, and she always cheers for her big brother. Aarav has a big dream: he wants to grow up to be clever, kind and successful.

 Every big journey starts with one curious question.

Think & Talk: What is one big dream you have?

 Page 3 — The Dream Bicycle

Illustration: Street outside City Cycles. Aarav cheers at the shiny red bicycle in the window (price tag ₹5,000); Anya watches.

Speech bubbles: “Wow! I really want this bicycle!”

One sunny afternoon, Aarav and Anya walk past a bicycle shop. In the window stands a shiny red bicycle with a silver bell. Aarav presses his nose against the glass.

Then he sees the price tag: ₹5,000. “That’s a lot of money,” he whispers. “How can I ever get it?”

 A dream is the first step. A plan is the second.

Think & Talk: Is there something you really, really want? Why do you want it?

 Page 4 — Needs vs Wants

Illustration: Dad points to two baskets on a table: NEEDS (apple, clothes, medicine, book, water) and WANTS (game controller, toy car, ice cream, teddy).

Speech bubbles: “We take care of needs first. Then we can plan for wants.” · “Hmm... so a bicycle is a want!”

At dinner, Aarav asks Dad to buy the bicycle. Dad smiles. “First, let’s learn something important,” he says. “There are needs, and there are wants.”

- Needs: Things we must have to live and stay healthy: food, water, a home, clothes, medicine and school books.
- Wants: Things that are nice to have, but we can live without: toys, video games, sweets and a new bicycle.

 Needs come first. Wants can wait.

Think & Talk: Is an umbrella a need or a want on a rainy day? What about on a sunny day?

 Page 5 — Meet the Piggy Bank

Illustration: Aarav’s bedroom at night. Anya hands Aarav the pink piggy bank.

Speech bubbles: “Let’s start saving! This is your little money helper.” · “Thank you, Anya!”

That evening, Anya runs into Aarav’s room holding something pink and round. It is her old piggy bank. “You can have it,” she says. “I’m getting a new one for my birthday!”

Aarav finds three coins in his pocket. Clink, clink, clink! They drop inside. It is the very first step towards his bicycle.

 Every big savings pot starts with one small coin.

Think & Talk: Where could you keep your savings safe at home?

 Page 6 — Why Saving Matters

Illustration: Dad and Aarav on the sofa. Coin stacks grow taller towards a “My Goal” bicycle poster.

Speech bubbles: “Saving means keeping some money now so you can use it later.”

“Dad, why should I save?” asks Aarav. “Can’t I just ask you for money when I need it?”

Dad points to the coins on the table, from the smallest pile to the tallest. “Saving gives you freedom,” he says. “It helps you reach your goals, it keeps you ready for surprises, and it lets you make your own choices.”

 Save a little today. Smile a lot tomorrow.

Think & Talk: What kind of surprise might you need savings for?

 Page 7 — The Three Money Jars

Illustration: Three glass jars labelled SAVE, SPEND and SHARE. Aarav holds a coin; Dad watches proudly.

Speech bubbles: “So every coin has a job!”

The next day, Dad brings three glass jars and helps Aarav label them. “Whenever you get money,” Dad says, “split it into three parts.”

- Save: For future goals, like the bicycle.
- Spend: For small things now, like a pencil or a treat.
- Share: To help others or to give a gift.

 Save, spend and share — and your money will be wise and kind.

Think & Talk: If you had ₹10, how would you split it between the three jars?

 Page 8 — A Visit to the Bank

Illustration: Bank interior with a vault door. Mrs Kapoor welcomes Dad and Aarav at her desk.

Speech bubbles: “Welcome, Aarav! A bank keeps your money safe.”

On Saturday, Dad takes Aarav to the bank. A friendly manager called Mrs Kapoor welcomes them.

“A bank keeps people’s money safe,” she explains. “It also helps families save for big things, like education or a home.” With Dad’s help, Aarav opens his very first savings account. He feels very grown-up!

Note: Grown-up tip: Children’s savings accounts are opened and managed with a parent or guardian.

 A bank is a safe home for your savings.

 Page 9 — How Money Can Grow

Illustration: Mrs Kapoor points to a board: ₹100 → ₹110 → ₹121 with growing seedlings, labelled “A pretend example”.

Speech bubbles: “Interest is money your money earns.”

Mrs Kapoor draws a little picture. “When you keep money in a savings account, the bank adds a small extra amount. This extra money is called interest.”

Let’s pretend Aarav keeps ₹100 in the bank, and the bank adds ₹10 after one year. Now he has ₹110! The next year, interest is added to the whole ₹110, so he has ₹121. His money grew while he waited!

Note: This is a pretend example with easy numbers. Real banks usually add less than this, and interest rates can go up and down.

 Money can work for you when you save it wisely.

 Page 10 — A Small Mistake

Illustration: Bedroom at night. Aarav sits on his bed with a game console, worried. The piggy bank is nearly empty; a thought cloud shows the bicycle far away.

Speech bubbles: “Oh no! Now my bicycle will take much longer.”

A few weeks later, a friend shows Aarav a new video game. “Everyone has it!” says his friend. Without stopping to think, Aarav opens his piggy bank and buys the game.

For one evening, he plays and plays. But at bedtime, he looks at his piggy bank. It feels very light.

 A quick “yes” can turn into a long “oh no”.

Think & Talk: Have you ever bought something and wished you hadn’t?

 Page 11 — The Power of Patience

Illustration: Garden. Dad waters a newly planted mango seedling; Aarav smiles. A grown mango tree stands nearby.

Speech bubbles: “Everyone makes mistakes. Let’s learn from this one.”

The next morning, Dad finds Aarav looking sad. He doesn’t scold him. Instead, they plant a mango seed together. “This seed won’t become a tree tomorrow,” says Dad. “It needs water and time. Savings are just the same.” Then Dad teaches Aarav a simple rule.

The Pause Rule:
1. Stop — Don’t buy it straight away.
2. Think — Is it a need or a want? Does it help my goal?
3. Wait — Wait one day. If you still want it, talk to a grown-up.

 Patience turns small efforts into big results.

 Page 12 — A Disciplined Friend

Illustration: Green Park in the morning. Rohan (red cap) explains his routine to Aarav.

Speech bubbles: “I wake up early, do the hard things first, and then I play!”

Aarav’s best friend, Rohan, always seems to have time for everything. He finishes his homework, reads every day and still plays football in the park!

“How do you do it?” asks Aarav. Rohan grins. “I follow a simple routine. Discipline means doing what you planned, even when it’s hard.”

 Small habits create big futures.

Think & Talk: What is one thing you do every single day?

 Page 13 — Good Habits vs Bad Habits

Illustration: A crossroads: GOOD HABITS signpost points to a sunny side (book, apple, exercise, piggy bank); BAD HABITS points to a grey, rainy side (screen, sweets, late night, games).

That night, Aarav makes two lists. He wants to see which habits help him grow, and which ones slow him down.

Good habits help me grow: Waking up on time; Reading every day; Saving some money; Playing outside and exercising; Finishing homework on time; Eating fruit and vegetables.

Bad habits slow me down: Too much time on screens; Spending without thinking; Putting things off until later; Going to bed late; Eating too much junk food; Giving up too quickly.

 Choose the habits that make you stronger.

 Page 14 — Why Reading Books Matters

Illustration: Aarav reads under a big tree; his imagination flies out of the book: lightbulb, star, rocket, planet, castle.

Speech bubbles: “Books are like a teacher you can carry anywhere!”

Rohan lends Aarav a book about a girl who builds a rocket. Aarav reads it under the big tree in the park. Suddenly, he is flying past planets and visiting castles!

Books take Aarav to new places. They give him new words, new ideas and new ways to think.

Benefits: New ideas · Better thinking · More words · Big dreams

 Knowledge is an investment that never goes to waste.

Think & Talk: What is your favourite book, and why?

 Page 15 — Setting Smart Goals

Illustration: Aarav at his desk below his “MY GOAL” poster and a 10-step savings thermometer.

Speech bubbles: “Now I have a plan!”

Aarav decides it is time for a clear plan. He takes a big sheet of paper and writes down his goal. A smart goal says exactly what you want, how much you need and when you will get there.

Aarav still has ₹1,000 in his piggy bank, so he needs ₹4,000 more. If he saves ₹400 every month, he will reach his goal in 10 months.

 Don’t just dream. Take one small step.

Think & Talk: Can you check Aarav’s maths? What is ₹400 × 10?

 Page 16 — Learning Time Management

Illustration: Aarav points to his “MY DAY” planner with ticks; wall clock and alarm clock.

Speech bubbles: “When I plan my time, I have time to play too!”

Aarav realises that time is a lot like money. If he doesn’t plan it, it slips away! So he makes a daily plan and sticks it on his wall.

- 6:30 am — Wake up and stretch
- 7:00 am — Get ready for school
- 4:00 pm — Snack and homework
- 5:00 pm — Play outside
- 6:30 pm — Read a book
- 7:00 pm — Help at home and family time
- 7:45 pm — Dinner
- 8:30 pm — Lights out and sleep

 Your time is more valuable than gold.

 Page 17 — Becoming a Good Leader

Illustration: Classroom. Ms Rao looks on as Aarav, holding a clipboard, asks his team (Meera, Kabir, Rohan) for ideas.

Speech bubbles: “What ideas do you all have?”

At school, Ms Rao, the class teacher, asks Aarav to lead a team for the science fair. Aarav feels nervous. What if he gets it wrong?

Then he remembers what Dad told him: a good leader listens first. So he asks each team member what they enjoy doing. Soon, everyone has a job that suits them.

 A good leader helps everyone do their best.

Think & Talk: When have you helped a group work together?

 Page 18 — The Power of Teamwork

Illustration: The team builds a wind-turbine model village; Kabir cheers as the bulb lights up.

Speech bubbles: “Look! The bulb is glowing!”

The team builds a model of a wind turbine that lights up a tiny village. Meera paints the houses. Kabir connects the wires. Rohan builds the turbine. Aarav keeps the plan and checks that everyone has what they need.

When something gets stuck, they solve the problem together.

 Together, we can do more than any of us can alone.

Think & Talk: What job would you choose in Aarav’s team?

 Page 19 — Learning to Communicate

Illustration: The team sits on a rug. Meera holds a blue house, Kabir a red one; Aarav helps them take turns.

Speech bubbles: “Blue houses!” · “Red houses!” · “Let’s take turns and find an idea we all like!”

Sometimes the team doesn’t agree. Meera wants blue houses, and Kabir wants red ones! Aarav helps everyone talk it through. They take turns, listen carefully and find an answer they all like: blue and red houses!

1. Listen carefully.
2. Speak clearly and calmly.
3. Be kind and respectful.
4. Share your ideas with confidence.

 Good communication builds strong friendships.

 Page 20 — Facing Failure

Illustration: Science fair. Another team cheers with the trophy on stage; Aarav’s team stands sadly by their model.

Speech bubbles: “We worked so hard... and we still lost.”

On the day of the science fair, the team proudly shows its model. But the judges give the trophy to another team.

Aarav’s shoulders drop. Meera wipes away a tear. Even Rohan is quiet. It is okay to feel sad. Big feelings are part of trying something hard.

 It’s okay to feel sad. Then we can try again.

Think & Talk: What helps you feel better when things go wrong?

 Page 21 — Turning Mistakes into Lessons

Illustration: Ms Rao points to a board: “What went well” and “Next time we will...”. The team listens.

Speech bubbles: “Failure is not the opposite of success. It’s part of it.”

The next day, Ms Rao sits down with the team. Together, they make two lists: what went well, and what they can do better next time.

Their turbine worked beautifully, but they ran out of time to explain their idea clearly. Next time, they will practise their talk!

 Mistakes are lessons in disguise.

Think & Talk: Think of a mistake you made. What did it teach you?

 Page 22 — Helping Someone in Need

Illustration: Outside school. Aarav gives Ishaan new notebooks and books; Ishaan smiles.

Speech bubbles: “Thank you, Aarav! You’re a true friend.”

One morning, Aarav notices his classmate Ishaan sitting alone. Ishaan’s schoolbooks are old and torn, and his family can’t buy new ones right now.

Aarav remembers his Share jar. With Mum’s help, he buys two new notebooks, and he gives Ishaan some of his own books that are still in good condition. He does it quietly and kindly, just like a good friend should.

 Real wealth isn’t just what you have. It’s also what you share.

Think & Talk: Who could you help this week, and how?

 Page 23 — A Little Business

Illustration: School fair stall: “Handmade Bookmarks ₹10 each”. Aarav and Anya sell; Mum keeps the notebook; Kabir buys one.

Speech bubbles: “Bookmarks! Only ₹10 each!”

Aarav has an idea. He loves drawing, and his classmates love reading. Why not make bookmarks? With Mum’s help, he spends ₹100 on card and colours. He and Anya make 30 colourful bookmarks and sell them at the school fair for ₹10 each. Mum helps them write down every rupee.

- Money in: 30 bookmarks × ₹10 = ₹300
- Money out: Card and colours = ₹100
- Profit: Money left over = ₹200

Note: Grown-up tip: Children’s business activities should always be supervised by a parent or guardian.

 Solve a small problem, and you create value.

 Page 24 — Making a Smart Decision

Illustration: Aarav holds a coin beside three jars tagged ₹140, ₹40 and ₹20. A thought cloud crosses out the game and ticks the bicycle.

Speech bubbles: “My bicycle is my big goal. The game can wait!”

Aarav counts his profit: ₹200! Then a friend tells him about another new game. For a moment, Aarav is tempted. But he remembers the Pause Rule: stop, think, wait.

The next day, he makes his choice. He puts ₹140 in his Save jar, ₹40 in his Spend jar and ₹20 in his Share jar.

 A smart decision today brings you closer to your big goal.

Think & Talk: Do Aarav’s three amounts add up to ₹200?

 Page 25 — The Dream Comes True

Illustration: Outside City Cycles. Aarav in a yellow helmet cheers beside his new bicycle; Anya, Dad and Mum celebrate in confetti.

Speech bubbles: “I did it! I really did it!”

Months pass. Aarav saves his pocket money, his birthday money and his bookmark profits. Coin by coin, his savings grow. At last, he counts them with Dad: ₹5,000!

The whole family goes to the shop together, and Aarav buys the shiny red bicycle. Mum and Dad give him a helmet to keep him safe. He feels proud — not just because of the bicycle, but because of everything he learned along the way.

 Big dreams come true one small step at a time.

 Page 26 — 10 Golden Life Lessons

Illustration: Aarav and Anya stand on a stack of books holding a golden star, with a trophy, piggy bank and coins.

1. Know the difference between needs and wants.
2. Save first, spend wisely and always share.
3. Be patient: good things take time.
4. Pause before you buy: stop, think, wait.
5. Build good habits, one day at a time.
6. Read books and never stop learning.
7. Set clear goals and plan your time.
8. Lead by listening, and work as a team.
9. Learn from mistakes and never give up.
10. Be kind, honest and helpful to others.

Note: Money is a helpful tool. But family, health, kindness and learning are the greatest treasures of all.

 Being rich starts with making wise choices.

 Page 27 — Activities and Challenges

Illustration: Aarav and Anya work on activities at a table with pencils, scissors and a jar.

Four activities: Needs or Wants? (write N or W); Money Maths (₹50 split: half Save, ₹15 Spend, rest Share — answers ₹25/₹15/₹10); The 7-Day Challenge; Draw Your Dream.

 Page 28 — Parent–Child Discussion

Illustration: The family sits together on a park bench at sunset.

Stories are even better when we talk about them together. Choose a few questions and share your own answers too.

1. What was Aarav’s biggest lesson? What was yours?
2. What is the difference between a need and a want? Can you name three of each?
3. Why did Aarav feel sad after buying the game? What would you have done?
4. What goal would you like to save for? How could you plan for it?
5. Which good habit would you like to start this week?
6. How can you be a good leader at home or at school?
7. Who could you help this month, and how?

Grown-up tip: Try giving your child a small, regular amount of pocket money and three jars. Let them make small choices — and small mistakes — while the stakes are low.

Let’s learn, grow and build a brighter future together!

Can Aarav save enough for his dream?

Aarav dreams of a shiny red bicycle — but it costs ₹5,000! With a pink piggy bank, three money jars, a helpful dad and a few mistakes along the way, Aarav discovers how saving, patience, teamwork and kindness can turn a big dream into reality.

Inside, children discover: Needs vs wants, saving and sharing; Simple budgeting and goal setting; Good habits and time management; Leadership, teamwork and kindness; Learning from mistakes; 6 printable activity worksheets.

A warm story about money, habits and kindness for ages 6–10.
